How it works

  • Hard gates first. A place is ruled out, with the reason shown, when it is too far, has too little terrain above your minimum elevation, has a pass closure likely, or (in backcountry mode) has High or Extreme avalanche danger or no avalanche forecast. Resort and nordic modes show avalanche danger as a note instead.
  • Then a weighted score. Fresh snow, snow quality (snow-to-liquid ratio), wind, visibility and cloud, vertical, drive time and road reliability are each scored 0 to 1 and averaged with the weights you set.
  • Where the data come from (beta): the National Blend of Models for snow (24-hour median and range), snow-to-liquid ratio, snow level, wind, cloud and visibility at one point near each area, for the three days of the weekend. Pass delay chances come from seven winters of I-90 delays (WSDOT Snoqualmie Winter Operations Study) applied to the other highway passes, and the Paradise road follows the National Park Service’s winter opening rules. A bot also logs pass closures and the Paradise gate each morning so we can improve this. Not connected yet: NWAC avalanche forecasts (they start in late November, so backcountry mode is blocked until then) and live traffic. Drive times are estimates.
  • Checking itself: once real data flows, we will score its past picks against what was observed, the way the forecast evaluation scores our snowfall ranges.
