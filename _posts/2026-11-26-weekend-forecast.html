1. Recap

Happy Thanksgiving to all our CMW friends, new and old! We hope you had a wonderful holiday. Now, the goods. This past week was rough on mid-elevation terrain. Last weekend's cool air got shoved aside, like your uncle in the Turkey Bowl, by the first of two atmospheric rivers, which brought heavy rain to the southern Olympics and Puget Sound lowlands. With southerly winds running parallel to the Cascades, most of the range missed out on orographic lift (terrain forcing air upward). The upside: easterly flow through the passes let several of them hang onto below-freezing temperatures, even if little precipitation fell.

2. Synoptic Overview

Our Thanksgiving storm was a cornucopia of weather: high winds, intense rain rates and post-frontal convection, including a tornado warning in King County! A deep low west of Haida Gwaii pumped subtropical moisture into the region before the cold front swept east Thursday. Upper-level dynamics helped, too. A jet streak (a core of unusually strong winds within the jet stream) was aimed at northern Washington, and with some back-of-envelope physics we can show its left exit region (the northeast side) favors rising air, right over our mountains. Combined with terrain, that meant heavy precipitation. Stevens made the most of it: the Cowboy Mountain snow stake recorded over 13 inches through Thursday morning.

3. Local Forecast

As a transient ridge approaches from the west, a weak shortwave trough slides down its leading edge Friday, bringing another round of showers and mid-elevation snow as snow levels fall toward 4,000 feet. There's a north–south temperature gradient, so expect colder air and lower snow levels in the north. The ridge moves overhead Saturday, with possible early sun breaks and a chilly morning. Enjoy it while it lasts: clouds will increase as an intensifying system approaches, arriving Sunday afternoon.

4. Snow Forecast

Snow levels hover around 4,000 feet Friday and drop toward 3,000 feet by Saturday morning. They climb again later Saturday as warm air moves in ahead of Sunday's system. 5. Mid-Term

Precipitation likely begins Sunday afternoon as a deepening low approaches from the south. The image below (courtesy of AI Weahter Guy) shows the surface pressure "spaghetti plot" of solutions from each Google NextGen3 ensemble member. Less spread equals more confidence in location. Wind is the headline to start the week: gusts approaching 50 mph along the coast and in the mountains, and over 30 mph in the lowlands. Broad troughing looks to keep things unsettled into early next week, though the models still differ on the details, so expect more quick-passing systems, rather than any extended break. Gotta love PNW fall!

6. Outlook

Our frenemy, the Gulf of Alaska trough, is finally retreating. Troughing looks to set up over the Bering Sea instead, tapping a deep pool of cold air from Kamchatka and eastern Siberia. Uncertainty is moderate, but some ensemble members (and the AI-GEFS mean) drive that cold air across the Pacific, where it would pick up moisture and could bring cooler, snowier systems later next week. In classic weatherman fashion, we'll have to wait and see, but December could get off to a solid start.

This is low-confidence territory. Treat it as a hint about the pattern, not a forecast of any single storm.
